Consider two gases, $A$ and $B$, that are free to exchange energy and particles, such as in the following figure:

![[Diffusive Equilibrium]]

When both systems are in diffusive equilibrium, i.e. they do not want to exchange any more particles, we can say that their chemical potentials are equal:

$$
\mu_{A}=\mu_{B}
$$

With the chemical potential being defined as:

$$
\mu \equiv-T\left( \frac{ \partial S }{ \partial N }  \right)_{U,V}
$$

Particles tend to flow from higher chemical potentials to lower chemical potentials.

We could also write, for a constant entropy $S$ and volume $V$:

$$
\mu=\left( \frac{ \partial U }{ \partial N }  \right)_{S,V}
$$

Which means that a system's energy changes by $\mu$ when you add a particle to it, keeping the entropy and volume fixed.
