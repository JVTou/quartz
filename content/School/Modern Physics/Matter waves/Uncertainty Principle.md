Because of a particle's wave nature, it is fundamentally impossible to determine both its position and momentum along an axis. Quantitatively, $\Delta x$ and $\Delta p$ cannot be 0 simultaneously. This is Heisenberg's uncertainty principle:

$$
\Delta p_{x}\Delta x\geq \frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

# Applications

When we look at an atom's electrons, we know that they can be found within the atom's confines. This restricts the position uncertainty $\Delta x$ to a certain maximum, while simultaneously setting a large minimum the the momentum uncertainty $\Delta p_{x}$.
